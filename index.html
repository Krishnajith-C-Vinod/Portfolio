About Me

I'm from Kozhikode, Kerala, and a PhD scholar in Physics at the Indian Institute of Technology Madras, where I'm part of Teraqata, Dr. Ayana Ghosh's research group.

My research sits where theoretical condensed matter physics meets machine learning. I'm interested in how order, symmetry breaking and collective excitations emerge from microscopic models, and in how data-driven methods can learn structure–property relationships, predict the response of materials and help uncover interpretable physical laws.

Before this, I worked in quantum information, building machine-learned adaptive teleportation protocols for noisy channels. Combining physical models with learning algorithms in that work now shapes how I approach materials and many-body problems.

Alongside research, I enjoy scientific outreach: explaining hard physics intuitively, and building visual, interactive science experiences in code, 3D and electronics.

01 — Research

Where many-body physics
meets machine learning

My interests span analytical and computational condensed matter theory and the machine-learning methods that are changing how we study materials.

Theoretical Condensed Matter

Lattice models, electronic band structure, symmetry breaking and emergent collective excitations in correlated and ordered phases of matter.

Machine Learning for Materials

Learning structure → property maps: predicting electronic, optical and functional response of materials directly from crystal structures, spectra and images.

AI for Physics Discovery

Interpretable models and symbolic regression that recover physical laws from data, and testing whether learning systems can reason about physics beyond what they were trained on.

Computational & Data-Driven Physics

Numerical simulation, curated datasets and HPC workflows that connect theory, computation and experiment.
